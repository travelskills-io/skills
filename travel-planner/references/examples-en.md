# Examples and conventions: English, American clients

Read this file for every deliverable written in English for American clients. It holds the conventions, the phrase lists and one before/after per pattern of `patterns.md`.

Facts quoted in the examples (prices, hours, travel times) were checked on Nov 7, 2026, except where a "For the planner" note says otherwise. They show the form. Check them again before reusing them.

## English conventions

**Money.** Fees, budgets and estimates the client pays the advisor are in US dollars: "$450", "$2,800 to $4,500". Prices paid on the spot stay in the local currency, as charged: "€22". No currency conversion unless the user gives the rate.

**Times and dates.** "9 a.m.", "2:45 p.m.", "noon". Month before day: "June 12", "Tuesday, June 12". Driving distances in miles, temperatures in °F, walking distances as times ("about a 10-minute walk").

**Tone.** First person, warm, direct, contractions welcome ("I've", "you'll"). Open with the client's first name ("Hi Margaret,"). Close simply ("Talk soon," or "Best,") and the advisor's first name. No "kindly", no "please do not hesitate to contact me", no "rest assured".

**The advisor's reality.**
- Many American advisors book for their clients, through a host agency or their own agency. When the user books, "booked" and "confirmed" are normal words (see step 1 of `SKILL.md`).
- A planning fee is common: a flat amount, stated before the work starts, often nonrefundable. It is separate from supplier commissions.
- Supplier commissions exist. Disclose them when the user asks or when the client asks how the advisor is paid.

**Phrases to watch.**
- Brochure clichés (pattern 2): nestled, tucked away, breathtaking, stunning, hidden gem, must-see, bucket-list, once-in-a-lifetime, unforgettable, a perfect blend of old and new, paradise, oasis, something for everyone.
- Sales pressure (pattern 4): book now, don't miss out, lock in, limited time, exclusive deal, act fast, spots are filling up (without a source).
- Exclusive luxury (pattern 6): indulge, the ultimate, VIP, curated luxury, elevated experience, world-class.

## 1. The encyclopedic travel document

**Before:**

> Japan is an island nation of more than 14,000 islands with a history stretching back thousands of years. From ancient temples to neon-lit cities, it offers a perfect blend of old and new. Here's everything you need to know before your 15-day adventure...

**After:**

> **Day 1 · Tokyo.** You land at Narita. Take the Narita Express to Shinjuku: about 1 hour 30 minutes, ¥3,330 (fare checked Nov 7, 2026). Check in at Hotel Gracery. Your evening is free. I'd walk over to Omoide Yokocho, 5 to 10 minutes away, for yakitori at one of the tiny counters. It's an easy first night after a long flight.

For the planner: walk from the hotel to Omoide Yokocho not verified.

## 2. Generic destination descriptions

**Before:**

> Nestled in the heart of Tuscany, this stunning boutique hotel offers an unforgettable experience. Unwind by the breathtaking infinity pool and indulge in world-class cuisine.

**After:**

> I picked this guesthouse for you because you asked for quiet and vineyards, not a scene. It's a 20-minute drive from Cortona. The pool looks straight out over the vines, and it's nearly empty before 10 a.m., which fits your slow mornings. Dinner is family style with whatever the farm has that day. There's no menu, so tell me now about anything you don't eat.

**When the source holds no facts.**

Before:

> Tucked away in the Highlands, this charming cottage is the perfect getaway for nature lovers. A true oasis of calm, with must-see hikes right outside your door!

After (the planner's notes say the clients want to walk from the door and leave the car parked):

To the planner, first: the source gives no facts about the cottage, so here is a short holding text.

> You told me you want to park the car when you arrive and walk from the door, and that's what I'm checking this cottage against. I'm confirming which walks start right from the cottage, how long they are and how hard, and how far the nearest store is. Once I have those answers, you'll get your travel document with each walk laid out, plus how to reach the cottage without a car.

For the planner: walks that start at the cottage (distance and difficulty), nearest store, access without a car.

## 3. An itinerary with no margins or alternatives

**Before:**

> 9 a.m. Louvre · noon Lunch · 1 p.m. Eiffel Tower · 3 p.m. Seine cruise · 5 p.m. Montmartre

**After:**

> **9 a.m. · The Louvre.** €22 per adult (€32 for visitors from outside the European Economic Area, so €32 for you), free under 18. Book a time slot on the Louvre's official site. It's required, even for free tickets. Closed Tuesdays. Prices and rules checked Nov 7, 2026. Plan on about 2 1/2 to 3 hours.
> **12:30 p.m. · Lunch at the Palais-Royal,** about a 5-minute walk. I'd go to Café Kitsuné and eat in the garden.
> **2 p.m. · Walk** to Pont Alexandre III for photos, then along the Seine to Pont de l'Alma.
> **2:45 p.m. · Bateaux-Mouches cruise** from Pont de l'Alma, 1 hour 10 minutes. €17 online, €20 at the ticket office. From April to October, a boat leaves every 30 minutes, and your ticket works for any departure that day. Checked Nov 7, 2026.
> **4:30 p.m. · Option A:** Montmartre, by metro to Abbesses, about 30 minutes with one change. **Option B,** if you're tired: the Trocadéro gardens, about a 10-minute walk from the dock, for the view of the Eiffel Tower.

For the planner: not verified are the visit time at the Louvre, the walk from the Louvre to the Palais-Royal, Café Kitsuné's location in the Palais-Royal, whether it is takeaway, and its hours, the metro route to Abbesses, and the walks along the Seine and to the Trocadéro gardens.

## 4. Sales pressure instead of advice

**Before:**

> Don't miss out! Book now to lock in our exclusive partner rate before it's gone.

**After (the user books through a host agency):**

> If this hotel works for you, I can hold the room through my host agency. I'll send you the cancellation terms before anything is charged, so you can decide with the full picture.

## 5. One-size-fits-all content

**Before:**

> This trip has something for everyone, with activities for all ages and budgets.

**After:**

> I kept it to two activities a day, since your kids are 4 and 7. Past that, the afternoons fall apart. Mornings are for sightseeing, afternoons are pool time or a nap for whoever needs one. The restaurant on day 3 has a real kids' menu, which the first two don't.

## 6. Tailor-made presented as unaffordable

**Before:**

> Indulge in the ultimate luxury escape along the Amalfi Coast, with VIP service at every turn.

**After (illustrative amounts):**

> For 8 days on the Amalfi Coast in June, for the two of you, not counting flights from Chicago: my estimate is $2,800 to $4,500, depending on where you stay. A family-run guesthouse puts you near $2,800; a small hotel with a sea view, closer to $3,600. My planning fee is separate. I'll lay out both options side by side.

For the planner: estimate to validate before sending (assumptions: June, flights excluded, two levels of accommodation).

## 7. The catalogue proposal

**Before:**

> Your package includes round-trip airfare Boston to Lisbon, 7 nights at a 4-star hotel, airport transfers, a Sintra excursion and a fado dinner show.

**After:**

> You told me you wanted a city you can walk, no rental car, and one great night of music. So I put you in the Alfama: you can walk almost everywhere, but it's hilly, so pack good shoes. Sintra is day 4, by train, because that's the one day you wanted out of the city. For fado, I chose a small house on Thursday night instead of a dinner show, since you said you'd rather listen than dine.

## 8. The apologetic fee

**Before:**

> I know fees can feel like a lot, but mine are really reasonable! And since it's your first trip with me, I'm happy to knock a little off.

**After (amount given by the user):**

> My planning fee for your 12-day Italy trip is $450, due before I start. It covers our planning call, a day-by-day itinerary built around what you've told me, one round of changes and your final travel document. Bookings and on-trip costs are separate.

## 9. The unreadable travel document

**Before:**

> On your second day, after a relaxing breakfast, you might want to explore the old town with its charming little streets, and then if you feel like it head over to the covered market, which is open in the mornings, before catching the bus to the beach from the stop near the main square...

**After:**

> **Day 2 · Old town and beach**
> 9 a.m. · Old town on foot, starting at the main square
> 11 a.m. · Covered market (mornings only), about a 5-minute walk
> 1 p.m. · Bus to the beach from the main square, about 25 minutes; schedule on the bus company's website
> Plan B if it rains: the town museum, next to the bus stop

## Full example

**Before:**

> Welcome to Crete! This stunning island is a perfect blend of history and relaxation. Don't miss the Palace of Knossos, a true bucket-list site. Pristine beaches and world-famous cuisine await. There's something for everyone!

**After (family from Atlanta, kids 6 and 9):**

> **Day 3 · Knossos and the north coast**
> 8:30 a.m. · Leave the hotel, about 25 minutes by car.
> The site opens at 8 a.m. in summer, so you'll walk it before the heat. Plan on about 1 1/2 hours; with the kids, that's plenty. €20 per adult, free under 18. Buy tickets on hhticket.gr, the official site (price and hours checked Nov 7, 2026).
> On the way back, stop in Archanes, 10 minutes by car, for a snack on the square.
> Afternoon · Agia Pelagia beach.
> Plan B if the wind picks up: the Heraklion Archaeological Museum, about 30 minutes by car, where the original Knossos frescoes are. The kids get to see the real thing after the morning.

For the planner: not verified are the visit time at Knossos, the drives from the hotel to Knossos, to Agia Pelagia and to the museum, the museum's hours, and Knossos's hours if the trip falls outside April to October.
